Summary

A Contratto di Fiume (CdF) is a voluntary, negotiated agreement among public and private actors of a river basin (or lake, coast, aquifer) to carry out a shared action programme on water quality, flood risk and the use of the river territory. It runs on a fixed sequence of documents and a participatory process and, in the national definition, says nothing on legal force. That makes it both the setting where the PhD’s watershed games could be used and a real-world case of the soft-versus-binding question.

The claims rest on four primary documents read in full (the 2015 national definition, a 2023 policy note by the national network, the Aniene agreement text, the Serchio pilot report) and two journal abstracts. Most are produced by the bodies that promote CdF, so they describe the instrument as intended and say little about results.

Claims

Legal basis and definition

  1. Art. 68-bis of D.Lgs. 152/2006 was introduced by art. 59 of L. 221/2015 (Collegato Ambientale). As quoted, it makes CdF “strumenti volontari di programmazione strategica e negoziata” that contribute to district-level planning, aiming at water protection and management, enhancement of river territories and flood-risk safeguarding, “contribuendo allo sviluppo locale” (D.Lgs. 152-2006 art. 68-bis, quoted in Accordo CdF Aniene).
  2. The national reference document of 12 March 2015 defines CdF in the same terms. The parties define a shared Programma d’Azione (PA) “e si impegnano ad attuarlo attraverso la sottoscrizione di un accordo”. Lake, coast, transition-water, mouth and aquifer contracts count as CdF (Tavolo Nazionale CdF (2015)).
  3. The same document says CdF are meant as operational tools with concrete, monitorable results in the short and medium term, integrating existing plans, and are not new planning levels “che esauriscono la propria funzione con l’atto di sottoscrizione” (Tavolo Nazionale CdF (2015)).
  4. The coordinator of the national network describes a CdF as “un accordo volontario, tecnico e finanziario” between public and private partners at the scale of a coherent hydrographic unit, signed by basin authorities, Regions, Comuni, associations and entrepreneurs (Bastiani (2023)).
  5. Institutional timeline given by the network: founded 2007 within Agenda 21 Italy; Carta Nazionale 2010, adopted by most Regions; art. 68-bis in 2015; national observatory at the ministry in 2018; named in the Partnership Agreement 2021-2027. The 2015 document is called “la base metodologica adottata da tutti i CdF italiani” (Bastiani (2023), self-reported).

How a CdF is built (requirements 2.a to 2.h, Tavolo Nazionale CdF (2015))

  1. Documento d’intenti: motivations, general objectives, specific criticalities and shared working method. Its signature “dà avvio all’attivazione del CdF”.
  2. Analisi conoscitiva preliminare, integrated (environmental, social, economic), including a stakeholder analysis and the existing networks among stakeholders. One aim is to set operational objectives “sui quali i sottoscrittori devono impegnarsi”.
  3. Documento strategico: a medium- to long-term scenario combining district and wide-area planning with local development policies.
  4. Programma d’Azione with a “ben definito e limitato” horizon, “indicativamente di tre anni”. Afterwards the contract can be updated or a new PA approved, based on monitoring. For each action it states objectives, actors, “i rispettivi obblighi e impegni”, timing, human and economic resources and financial coverage.
  5. Open and inclusive participatory processes. Deliberation should be a balanced dialogue “che eviti squilibri a favore degli attori dotati di maggior peso politico ed economico”, among “soggetti liberi e uguali”, and participation “non va intesa come un semplice atto burocratico” (Tavolo Nazionale CdF (2015), footnote 9; repeated in Bastiani (2023)).
  6. A formal Atto di impegno, the Contratto di Fiume, that “contrattualizzi le decisioni condivise” and defines the “impegni specifici dei contraenti”. Then a periodic monitoring system covering implementation and “la qualità della partecipazione”, and public information (Tavolo Nazionale CdF (2015)).

How binding is it

  1. The 2015 document gives no view on the legal force of the Atto di impegno, on sanctions, or on non-delivery (Tavolo Nazionale CdF (2015)). The quoted text of art. 68-bis also contains none (D.Lgs. 152-2006 art. 68-bis).
  2. The Aniene CdF (Lazio) is drawn up both as a CdF under art. 68-bis and as an “Accordo di Programmazione Negoziata” under art. 2 c. 203 lett. a) of L. 662/96, defined as a “regolamentazione concordata” between public, or public and private, parties (Accordo CdF Aniene).
  3. Its commitments, in the articles read: each signatory is responsible for or involved in actions; all commit to seek funding “in forma solidale”; if funds are missing the responsible actor informs the Assemblea (Art. 5); the Comitato monitors at least yearly and the Assemblea discusses problems and acts “provvedendo a identificare eventuali misure correttive” (Art. 7); duration is five years (Art. 9); signatories may withdraw, and others join, at any time by a reasoned measure of their own body (Art. 11). No sanction or penalty clause appears in these articles; the annexes are not in the file (Accordo CdF Aniene).
  4. The Central Apennines district plan (PGDAC.2) gives “Contratti Territoriali” an implementing function: to catalyse stakeholders’ resources, involve them financially, and make them co-responsible for non-structural actions that bring “condizionamenti e limiti alle loro attività” (Accordo CdF Aniene, premises).

Scale and Tuscany

  1. By 2023 CdF existed in all Italian regions, with about 200 processes started and almost 80 signed (Bastiani (2023), self-reported).
  2. In Tuscany, 23 CdF were reported as started and 11 as signed, spread over the six regional Consorzi di Bonifica; the network coordinator is quoted saying that, if all were completed, they would cover about 40% of Tuscan Comuni, and the president of ANBI Toscana that without financial coverage “le migliori intenzioni condivise restano solo sulla carta” (gonews, regional assembly report; page not opened, figures taken from a search excerpt; undated).
  3. A Tuscany Region call for 2019-2021 had €275,000 over three years and funded 8 projects to enhance and redevelop watercourses in urban settings; one is Volterra’s “Verso il contratto di fiume della Val di Cecina” with five other Comuni (gonews; gonews, final ranking; headlines and search excerpts only, the pages could not be opened).
  4. Lucca’s Serchio pilot (February to April 2012, MED project WATERinCORE): 272 actors mapped, 10 events, over 350 attendances, a participatory SWOT, EASW visioning, 44 project ideas in five groups, a public vote with up to three preferences each (177 cast), ending in an Action Plan and a Protocollo d’Intesa (Lazzari et al. (2012)).
  5. Florence area: university action-research over five years in the Pesa valley aimed to implement the Arno “contract of contracts” and regional planning tools (Lingua (2025), abstract). The Pesa CdF is proposed as a regional landscape project, a strategic instrument of the landscape plan (Gisotti (2025), abstract).

Process length (Aniene)

  1. Public forums from 13 April 2002; association meetings towards a CdF from February 2014; Documento d’Intenti formalised on 19 July 2018; territorial tables from December 2018; last plenary of the participatory process on 25 January 2022 (Accordo CdF Aniene).

Inferences

  1. A CdF is a sequence of documents, so a game can be mapped to a phase. Our mapping to the games in Game Experiments for Contract-Making in Watersheds: the Water Trust Game (claim 3 there) diagnoses trust during the preliminary analysis (claim 7 here, stakeholder analysis and networks); Wat-A-Game (claim 9 there) supports participation and the strategic document (claims 8 and 10 here); the auction and contract experiments (family B there) help design payment clauses in the Programma d’Azione (claim 9 here). The Serchio sequence (SWOT, EASW visioning, flagship projects, public vote; claim 19) shows concretely what a game would have to fit into or replace.
  2. Soft versus binding is the same question as in the enforcement literature. The national text asks for commitments and states no legal force (claim 12); Aniene adds a second legal layer (claim 13) but allows free exit and shows no sanction (claim 14). Whether adding a legal layer helps or crowds out voluntary reciprocity is the question of Jack’s enforcement treatment and of Incentives, Payment Schemes and Crowding Out. A game could treat the strength of the commitment layer as a treatment: voluntary only, public monitoring, legal layer.
  3. Monitoring and publicity are the CdF’s own substitute for enforcement. Claims 11 and 14 rely on yearly monitoring, the assembly discussing problems and public information. Observability of upstream effort is also the design variable suggested by the Water Trust Game’s distrust finding (inference 1 of the games note). So “who sees what” is both a real CdF clause and a game variable.
  4. Balanced dialogue is a testable requirement. Claim 10 asks that powerful actors not dominate deliberation. A role-playing game with unequal endowments or roles (hill landowners, plain, beach businesses) can measure whether deliberation drifts towards the strong, which would make the game a measurement tool inside the CdF’s own quality criteria (monitoring “la qualità della partecipazione”, claim 11) and not something external to it.
  5. Camaiore is an atypical CdF. The cases here (Aniene with a whole river basin and many public bodies; Serchio with 7 Comuni and 272 actors) are multi-jurisdiction. If the Camaiore basin is roughly the municipality (see the context table in the games note), parties are user groups and the Comune, so the signatories and the “Assemblea” would look different. Existing CdF practice is a weak guide to who would sign.
  6. The fundable first step is a “Verso un CdF” process, not a signed contract. The Serchio pilot ended in a protocol of intent after three months of intense work (claim 19), and Tuscany has funded “Verso il contratto di fiume” projects (claim 18). A Comune-led proposal with the Consorzio di Bonifica Toscana Nord is our suggestion; no active Camaiore CdF has been found (claim 1 of the games note).
  7. Signature is too slow to be the outcome measure. Aniene took about 20 years from the first forum and 3.5 years from the Documento d’Intenti to the last plenary (claim 21), and nationally only about 40% of started processes are signed (claim 16, rough ratio of 80 to 200). A thesis needs earlier outcomes: behaviour in the process (attendance, commitments made, contributions) and not signature.
  8. Entry, exit and renewal are the real-world handover clauses. Aniene’s five-year term with renewal after monitoring (Art. 9), free withdrawal and accession (Art. 11) and the three-year PA horizon (claim 9) are how a CdF deals with newcomers and officeholders who did not write it. They are a natural source of rules for any game about contracts that outlive their authors.
  9. The PGDAC.2 wording is a bridge to payment clauses. Making stakeholders co-responsible for actions that restrict their activities (claim 15) is close to compensation for land-use restrictions. Whether any CdF action programme actually contains payments between parties is not known (see Open).

Limits

  • Promoter sources. The 2015 standard, the 2023 note and the Serchio report come from the bodies promoting CdF, and the Aniene file is the agreement text itself. They describe the instrument as intended. There is no independent evaluation of results here, so effectiveness is untested in this note.
  • Standard, not law. The 2015 document is a working-group quality standard and predates art. 68-bis. The statute is known only through a quotation in another document.
  • Aniene file is an unsigned template. Signatories, signature date and annexes (action sheets, funding, monitoring programme) are missing, so who committed to what cannot be checked.
  • Tuscan figures are thin. The 23/11 count and the 2019-2021 call come from news items I could not open (search excerpts only); the count is undated.
  • Two journal articles known by abstract only. The Contesti PDFs were blocked.
  • National counts are self-reported (2023), and “signed” covers different instruments.
  • Official view only. The critical Italian literature on participation and politicisation of river contracts (listed in the games note) is not integrated, so the note is probably too favourable to the instrument.
  • Our inferences are untested, in particular the mapping of games to phases (inference 1) and the transfer of Aniene and Serchio practice to Camaiore (inferences 5 and 6).

Open

  • Legal force. What does a signed CdF bind a party to, beyond its second instrument (accordo di programmazione negoziata, protocollo d’intesa)? Read the full text of art. 68-bis on Normattiva, and check whether Tuscany has its own regional rules.
  • Camaiore. Status of any CdF or “Verso un CdF” process: ask the Comune, the Consorzio di Bonifica Toscana Nord and the Region.
  • Payment clauses. Does any Italian CdF action programme contain compensation or payment between parties (PES-like)? Aniene’s annexes and Tuscan programmes (Elsa 2022-2025, Pesa) are the places to look.
  • Participation funding in Tuscany. The working claim that Tuscany’s participation authority (APP) has often funded participatory processes on rivers is not verified. Searches found that the APP supports local participatory processes under regional law 46/2013, and nothing specific to river contracts.
  • Origin. The working summary says the idea comes from the contrats de rivière of Belgium and France in the 1980s. Search results suggest France from 1981 and Wallonia only from the early 1990s; unchecked (see To be verified).
  • Registry. Is there a regional or national registry of CdF to settle the counts and the signed or started status?
