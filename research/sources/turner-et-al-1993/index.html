Exhaustible (Non-Renewable) & Renewable Resource Use

  • non renewable extraction is mainly concerned with optimal rate & amount
  • user cost → possible future use is eradicated by extraction in the present
  • total cost = extraction costs + user costs
    Shift from GDP-centered paradigm towards Post-Growth society
    → ability to balance human needs with planetary boundaries

We tend to regard costs and benefits in the future as being less important than now. (time preference?)

Carlisle (1954) question of OPTIMAL AMOUNT of extraction

Hotelling (1931) → free/easy access to a resource results in too rapid rate of extraction
OPEN ACCESS is possible e.g. because of lack of property rights.
Differentiate OPEN ACCESS PROBLEM to COMMONS PROBLEM, hence it is also linked to the PROBLEM of POLLUTION & ASSIMILATIVE CAPACITY ⇢ ability to absorb wastes without signs of excessive change and stress

Gordon (1954) → rules for optimal extraction over time of Renewable Resources. He compared open access to single ownership conditions → optimal amount & optimal timing are interdependent because of regeneration rates being a function of time.

Environmental Ethics

Intergenerational Social Contract:

  • same opportunities for the future generations like for the past generations
  • implies obligations of the current generation to future ones
  • traditional ethical reasoning needs to be broadened
    ↳ extension of moral reference class

“Intergenerational Equity Criterion”

DEEP ECOLOGY → reference class of ethics to cover also interests and rights of non-human nature, because non-human nature is inherently valuable (→Valuation).

“Intergenerational and Intragenerational Equity Objectives”

“Stewardship Ethics” may be sufficient for Sustainability → people should be less greedy because of the responsibility they have (instrumental value)

Gaianism: (Gaia hypothesis, 1972)
The system “Gaia” has developed so that it can regulate and repair itself → but the process of repair only guarantees the system’s survival and not the survival of any species.

Land Ethics (Leopold, 1949)

According to Norton (1990, 1992), Leopold’s ideas can be viewed as an argument for a

  • 2-level criterion of ECOSYSTEM HEALTH and a
  • 2-stage POLICY PROCESS
    ↳ 1. Limits need to be determined
    ↳ 2. Operation of subsystem on basis of resource conservation

Contractualism. Relying on contracts, but works only for parties of equal power: e.g. future generations have no power at all, they are completely vulnerable.

Rights. Relying on rights given rather than goods and bads. For future generations it works better but there is still the problem of Contingency of future generations.

Resourcism. Each generation should have the same amount/level of resources ⇢ What if we don’t know about regeneration rates? What if sharing non-renewable resources? What if technologies for extraction change?

Egalitarianism. Equality of some characteristic. “Difference principle” (Rawls). Generalised obligations rather than tied to individuals. Generations are not seperate, but overlap. → Chain of obligations.

Bioethics. Assuming 1) non-human beings have moral standing and 2) there are more beings with moral standings than overall conscious beings.

Teleology. Weighing up goods and bads while maximising goods. E.g. maximising wellbeing, i.e. ‘utility’ or preference satisfaction, is known as utilitarianism. Weighing one good to another good, e.g. equality vs. utility. Teleology may be inconsistent with itself.
↳ as used by cost-benefit-approach

Valuation

positive value → benefit → people feel better
negative value → cost → people feel worse

Economic valuation measures human preferences with respect to changes in the environment but it does not ‘value’ the environment itself.

Issues.

  • preference management is unreliable and not valid
  • fate of environments should not depend on human wants
  • human wants matter but are not the only source of value
    ↳ there is intrinsic value in nature

Intrinsic value. This is a ‘sterile’ debate, because it is not possible to show empirically ⇢ it has to be accepted or rejected intuitively.
