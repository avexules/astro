Equilibrium v7

Updated 2026-03-22

Equilibrium

7th Iteration. For 6 players.

Develop interest in sustainable resource distribution and its challenges in a game whose rules can be explained in 3 minutes or understood by reading instructions in 7 minutes!

Equilibrium is about the scarcity of water, migration of people, and redistribution of resrouces for sustainability and equality. Six societies need to cooperate to endure a heap of disastrous events – meanwhile, every society tries to generate a maximum of individual economic value.

Material

  • Society board: Each player has a small cardboard piece that holds their population dice. The starting value for each die is marked there. The neighbouring player are also depicted through colored arrows, defining the seating plan.

  • Population dice: 18 dice in 6 different colors (minimum population is 1-1-1, maximum is 6-6-6). Each player has 3 dice representing distinct socioeconomic classes: L (Low class), M (Middle class), H (High class).

  • Water tokens: XX blue tokens

  • Economy cards: Each player has 7 hand cards depending on their color which all show a:

    • Full value (large number): Points awarded at the end of the game if condition is met
    • Commission value (small number in parentheses): Payment received when card is used to enable redistribution or played in economy phase
    • Condition: Requirement that must be true about the card owner’s economy – if not precised, values relate to own dice, water, or game actions

  • Resolution die: 1 D20 to resolve any ties in events or conditions. E.g.: “player with highest total population” when three players are tied in having the highest population. The three players roll the die, the highest roll always counts.

Start

Each player receives their society board which defines their color and their neighbours. Adjust the seating order accordingly.

Each player receives 3 population dice (L, M, H) according to their assigned color, and sets them to their starting value marked on the society board.

Each player receives 7 economy cards as a secret hand according to their color.

Place XX water tokens in the common pool. Players take water from the pool in the following order. Each player decides how much to take. There are no restrictions on the amount taken.

  1. Pink
  2. Rose
  3. Orange
  4. Brown
  5. Black
  6. White

Player’s population dice (L, M, H) and water tokens are visible to everybody. The three class dice are kept in order on the society board. Population dice cannot be changed except in the Migration phase, Check phase, or when an event specifies.

Round phases

  1. Event phase Turn over the topmost card as the current rounds event. Players need to resolve the event collectively. Only when the event is resolved, they proceed. If the event card states “as redistribution” or “as migration”, the respective phase is skipped for this round.

  2. Redistribution phase (single) Players may request a redistribution of any amount of water tokens from any other player to them. Just a single redistribution per round is allowed and only if the event card is not resolving as redistribution.

    Cost: To redistribute X water tokens, anyone (the requester, the giver, or a third party) can pay with an economy card whose commission value is at least X. The condition of that card must be valid on the card owner’s economy (checking their L/M/H dice, water, and any relevant game actions).

    Reward: The player giving the water receives the commission value allocated i.e. keeps the used economy card face up in front of them (their “commission earnings”).

  3. Migration phase (multiple) Players that have more population than water can send up to 3 migrants to one of their neighbors (and only one). Multiple players can migrate per round but only if the even card is not resolving as migration.

    Emigration: The migrating player chooses which class(es) leave (L, M, or H dice). For example, they might choose L-2 (reducing the Low class die by 2), or L-1 and M-1 (reducing both by 1), or any combination. This represents selective emigration policy and strategically reshapes their population structure.

    Immigration: The receiving neighbor must give asylum if one of their die allows and chooses which single class accepts the migrants i.e. which die increases: L, M, or H. For example, receiving 2 migrants, they might choose H+2, or L+2 or M+2 (exclusive or). Dice cannot go below 1 or above 6. If no die can support the migrants within these limits, migration to this neighbor is not possible.

  4. Economy phase Each player may choose to secure one economy card from their hand by playing it face-up in front of them. The card’s condition must be valid on their current economy (checking their L/M/H dice, water, and any relevant game actions from this round). The player immediately earns the card’s commission value added to their commission earnings. This allows players to lock in economic value before the uncertainties of the Check phase, but at the cost of removing future options for enabling redistribution.

  5. Check phase Loss: Each player that has fewer water tokens than total population must reduce their population dice by the deficit amount (max -3 per round). The player chooses which class(es) decline: any combination of L, M, or H dice reductions totaling the deficit. For example, with deficit -2, they might choose L-2, or H-1 and M-1, or L-1 and H-1. This represents which classes suffer most during water scarcity.

    Gain: Each player that has more water tokens than total population may increase their population dice by the surplus amount (max +3 per round). The player chooses which class(es) grow: any combination of L, M, or H dice increases totaling the surplus. For example, with surplus +2, they might choose M+2 (middle class growth), or L+1 and H+1 (polarization), or any combination. This represents which classes benefit from prosperity.

    Dice constraints: All dice must remain between 1-6. If a player cannot accommodate the required changes within the limits, they make as many changes as possible.

End of the game A player ist out of the game if during Check phase ANY of their dice (L, M, or H) would drop below 1. Dice cannot go below 1, so any required reduction when a die is already at 1 means the game is over for that player. All water tokens and cards of that player are lost.

The game also ends when the last round was played. All players that remain after the last Check phase (i.e. that have survived and have all dice at least ≥1), calculate their Economy score to form a ranking:

Economy score = Commission earnings + Full earnings

  • Commission earnings: Sum of all commission values from economy cards they played face-up during Redistribution and Economy phases
  • Full earnings: Sum of full values from all economy cards remaining in their hand whose conditions are NOW met
